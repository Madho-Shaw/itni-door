The distance discount

Why do Indian families send daughters to study less far than sons with the same rank?

1. The gap

At the same entrance score, families send daughters to study far from home less often than sons. Each doubling of distance goes with -9.3% lower odds that a study migrant is a woman (Census 2011). A Maharashtra woman with the same MHT-CET score is 3.7 points less likely to study outside her home region (CAP 2023-25).

2. The textbook says: the same choice Class Becker's household

Becker's household picks the college with the best value of quality minus fees and fares. Same rank, same seats, same prices for a son and a daughter, so it predicts the same choice for both. Ability, price and lower returns for women all fail.

3. Our model Class safety and honour Class pink tax Class statistical discrimination

Parents choose a pair of colleges for twins at rank 8,000: both stay at NIT Patna, together at NIT Durgapur, best seats (he Durgapur, she Delhi), or only he goes. Score = value of the two seats minus money minus worry, and two costs fall only on her: an escort, and worry per hour it takes to reach her.
