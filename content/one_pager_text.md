# The distance discount

Why do Indian families send daughters to study less far than sons with the same rank?

Group 7, Mshaw, R K Arjun and Neil John Vivek. ECO463, Shiv Nadar University, November 2026. [QR code and short link to the hub]

## 1. The gap

In class we studied how some families cap a daughter's study radius at about 100 km, to keep her within reach. We put numbers on it. Each doubling of the distance of a study move goes with 9.3% lower odds that the mover is a woman (Census 2011, 960 state pairs). In Maharashtra, a woman with the same MHT-CET score is 3.7 points (about 14%) less likely than a man to study outside her home region (CAP 2023-25). JoSAA's choice lists by gender would test it directly, but our RTI request for them was refused.

## 2. The textbook model from class says the same choice

Becker's household weighs college quality against fares and fees, so with the same rank a son and a daughter should go equally far. Ability (same score bin), price (same fees and fares) and lower returns for her (in the same branch, Maharashtra women sit in slightly better colleges) all fail. "She prefers near" only restates the gap.

**The twist.** At rank 8,000 a Bihar son's best seat is NIT Durgapur CSE (median pay ₹11.0 lakh). His sister can take NIT Delhi CSE (₹15.4 lakh) through the women-only pool, which closes at 15,361 against 7,651 for him. So she should go far more surely than he does.

## 3. Our model: four choices, two costs only she carries

At choice filling, last year's closing ranks show which seats each twin can reach; parents pick a pair of colleges. Her say β is how much weight the family gives her view.

| Both stay | Together | Best seats | Only he goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Both at NIT Patna | Both at NIT Durgapur (she is the tied mover, Mincer 1978) | He Durgapur, she Delhi | He Durgapur, she Patna |

| Block, in plain words | Formula |
|---|---|
| Value: both children's pay, for H years of work after the degree | H × (his pay + her pay) |
| Money: fares, plus her escort's tickets and lost days | λ × [16 fares each + J × (2 fares + e × w)] |
| Worry: hours until her parents can reach her, times the worry per hour | [σ / (1 + n + b if her brother is there) + τ] × R, R = hours ÷ 24 |

Score = value − money − worry; the family picks the highest. The son has no escort and no worry term.

**Escort**, a "pink tax": the same seat costs her more to reach; zero when her brother is there, and small. **Worry** has a statistical part σ that information moves (a senior girl from her district is someone the parents can call) and a norm part τ, "log kya kahenge", that nothing moves. Results read as "out of 100 families like this".

| H | σ | τ | b | μ | λ | n | Day wage w | J |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 16, calibrated (town family on the fence) | 4 | 1 | 3 | 2.5 / 1.2 / 1.0 | 0 / 0 / 5 | ₹378.93 sourced / ₹2,000 / ₹4,167 | 8 train, 2 air, 0 with brother |

All assumed unless marked; pay, closing ranks and main travel legs are sourced.

## 4. Shalini, village in Saharsa, worked through

| ₹ lakh | Value | Money | Worry | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Together | 66.06 | 0.46 | 7.25 | 58.35 |
| Best seats | 79.17 | 1.56 | 22.08 | 55.53 |

Delhi is 26.5 hours away, so worry outweighs pay. The four-year travel bill for Best seats, ₹62,366, is under one year's pay gap between Delhi and Patna (₹6.42 lakh).

## 5. Out of 100 families like this

| Bihar family | Her best seat | His best seat | Most common |
|---|---|---|---|
| Village (Shalini) | 23 | 96 | Together, 59 |
| Begusarai town (Kajal) | 44 | 98 | Best seats, 44 |
| Patna city (Ira) | 98 | 100 | Best seats, 98 |

Kajal is calibrated. The order follows mostly from reach time (26.5, 21 and 5.75 hours). Ira's family pays most for travel (₹1,65,114) yet sends her: Delhi is one flight away. In the village she needs a say of at least 0.3 to get Delhi.

## 6. Predictions

Supported, the gap closes at top ranks (-4.0 / -3.0 / -1.2 points by score band). Not supported, airport distance at the college end (p = 0.63). Untested, sisters end at a brother's college more than their rank needs.

## 7. Fixes (her Delhi chance, village)

| Fix | Term | Effect | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pay the escort's fares | 2f | 23 to 26 | Money for a cost that barely binds |
| A senior girl from her district at the college | n | 23 to 85 | Slow where no girl has gone |
| A promised first-year hostel place for girls | σ | 23 to 85 | May move the watching to campus |

Sizes rest on our assumed 80/20 worry split; the ranking does not. No fix moves τ. Our limits are composite families, a calibrated σ, and H, which moves the levels (1 in 100 at H = 1, 88 at H = 5).
